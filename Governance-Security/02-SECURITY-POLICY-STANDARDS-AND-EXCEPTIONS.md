# Security policy, standards, and exception management

## Purpose and scope

A policy system turns leadership intent and legal obligations into clear, testable requirements. This guide explains the lifecycle for producing and operating policy, standards, procedures, and exceptions.

## Policy architecture

- **Enterprise security policy:** leadership intent, scope, accountability, and high-level mandatory outcomes.
- **Domain policies:** identity, data protection, incident response, asset management, supply-chain security, backup, and acceptable use.
- **Standards:** minimum measurable requirements.
- **Procedures:** repeatable steps, decision points, and escalation paths.
- **Guidance:** recommended implementation patterns.

## Lifecycle

1. Initiate and define scope.
2. Identify obligations and impacted teams.
3. Draft policy with clear requirements.
4. Review with stakeholders.
5. Approve with delegated authority.
6. Communicate and train users.
7. Implement controls and evidence collection.
8. Monitor compliance and exceptions.
9. Review, update, and retire when needed.

## Minimum policy contents

- Owner, approver, version, and review dates
- Scope, purpose, and definitions
- Mandatory requirements and roles
- Evidence expectations
- Escalation and exception path
- Training and communication plan
- Related standards and source obligations

## Exception management

An exception should be documented, time-bound, approved by the correct authority, supported by compensating controls, and reviewed before expiry. Exceptions are not a waiver of legal requirements unless a qualified authority confirms an allowed alternative.

## Policy metrics

Track control coverage, training completion, exception age, policy review currency, and repeat findings. Metrics are useful only when linked to scope and evidence.
