# Compliance assurance, control testing, and audit readiness

## Purpose

Assurance produces evidence that controls are designed and operating as intended. It does not guarantee that no incident will happen.

## Assurance cycle

Define scope and criteria → identify owners → collect evidence → test design and operation → document results and limitations → assign actions → remediate and validate → report and retain evidence.

## Control description

For each control, document:

- purpose and risk addressed
- mapped obligations
- scope and owner
- operating frequency
- dependencies
- evidence and records
- failure and escalation path
- test criteria and review date

## Testing approaches

- design assessment
- implementation assessment
- operating-effectiveness testing
- automated-control validation
- independent audit or attestation

## Evidence handling

Evidence should be current, relevant, attributable, and protected from unauthorized modification. Good evidence includes date, source, scope, owner, and retention status.

## Findings and remediation

A finding should explain the requirement, what was observed, likely cause, impact, action plan, and validation method. Closure requires more than a ticket being marked complete.

## Audit readiness checklist

- owners are clear
- evidence is current and indexed
- exceptions are visible and time bound
- findings have tracked remediation
- access to evidence is controlled
- scope and limitations are explicit
