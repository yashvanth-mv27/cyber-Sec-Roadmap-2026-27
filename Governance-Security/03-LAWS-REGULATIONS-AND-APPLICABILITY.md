# Laws, regulations, contracts, and framework applicability

## Why this matters

Security obligations come from laws, regulations, industry requirements, contracts, customer commitments, and internal policy. Applicability depends on facts such as location, data, entity type, and service context.

## How to assess an obligation

Document:

- who is in scope
- where processing occurs
- what data or service is involved
- which law, contract, or framework may apply
- what task or control outcome is required
- who owns interpretation and implementation
- when the obligation takes effect and how it is reviewed

## Common domains

- Privacy and personal data protection
- Health information security
- Payment and financial controls
- Critical infrastructure and sector-specific rules
- Government and defense contract obligations
- Supplier and customer security commitments
- Framework adoption such as NIST, ISO, CIS, SOC 2, and COBIT

## Mapping to controls

Maintain a controlled register that links obligations to affected services, owners, risk scenarios, control objectives, evidence references, and review dates. One control can satisfy multiple obligations, but the mapping must remain traceable per requirement.

## Change monitoring

Track updates in law, regulator guidance, contract clauses, and standards. Reassess changes through a formal triage and action plan with an accountable owner.

## Reporting and decision discipline

Do not treat an external standard as legal advice or assume it always applies. Claims of compliance must match the exact scope, period, and criteria.
