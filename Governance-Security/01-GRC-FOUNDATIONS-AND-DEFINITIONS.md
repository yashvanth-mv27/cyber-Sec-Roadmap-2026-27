# GRC foundations and shared vocabulary

## Purpose

This guide establishes a consistent vocabulary for governance, risk, and compliance (GRC) work in a security program. Use it to help technical, legal, privacy, audit, and business teams discuss the same obligations and evidence. It is an educational reference, not legal advice or a statement that an organization is compliant.

## Core terms

- **Governance:** The structures, decision rights, oversight, and accountability used to direct security toward business objectives.
- **Risk:** The effect of uncertainty on objectives. A cyber-risk statement should identify a cause or scenario, an affected asset or process, and a plausible business consequence.
- **Inherent risk:** Risk before considering controls.
- **Residual risk:** Risk remaining after controls and treatment are considered.
- **Risk appetite / tolerance:** The amount or type of risk leadership is willing to pursue or retain, and the boundaries that trigger escalation.
- **Control:** A safeguard or activity intended to modify risk.
- **Compliance:** Demonstrable conformance with applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, policy, or framework requirements.
- **Assurance:** A reasoned conclusion supported by evidence that a control or process is effective.
- **Finding:** A documented gap between an expected requirement and observed condition.
- **Exception:** A formally approved deviation from a requirement with compensating controls and expiry.
- **Evidence:** Records that support conclusions about control operation or decision-making.
- **Obligation:** A requirement from law, regulation, contract, policy, or framework.
- **Risk owner:** The accountable leader for understanding and deciding how a risk is treated.
- **Control owner:** The accountable role for design, operation, evidence, and remediation.

## Requirement hierarchy

1. External obligation
2. Policy
3. Standard
4. Procedure / playbook
5. Guidance
6. Evidence and record

## GRC lifecycle

Understand context → identify obligations and risks → set objectives → design controls → operate controls → collect evidence → assess and report → remediate or accept residual risk → review and improve.

## Minimum records for a decision

A risk or compliance decision should include: scope, assumptions, source requirement or scenario, evidence reviewed, impact and likelihood rationale, options considered, owners, approval, residual risk, action plan, and review date.

## Working principles

- Assign one accountable owner.
- Separate facts, assumptions, and interpretations.
- Map controls to requirements and risks with traceability.
- Do not claim compliance or effectiveness based only on documentation.
- Reassess after changes, incidents, or new obligations.

## Learning exercise

Select a fictional service and document one risk, one control objective, one risk owner, one control owner, and the evidence that would prove the control is working.
